Research

Across molecules, neural circuits, and behavior, I use computational models to ask what observed patterns can—and cannot—tell us about biological systems.

Research Focus

01 — neural signals

I am especially interested in analyses that keep neural dynamics tied to behavior, rather than treating signal processing as a detached technical exercise.

02 — interpretable models

I care about models most when they help clarify what is being learned, which is why interpretability has become central rather than optional in my ML work.

03 — reproducible workflows

A recurring part of my work is turning messy analysis into reusable structure: cleaner code, better figures, and pipelines that remain legible when the question evolves.

Virtual-Rat Gap Crossing

Question: What does a reinforcement-learning policy use to cross gaps, and can it learn when not to jump?

Approach: Varied rewards, visual input, gap width, starting position, and evaluation conditions in a physically simulated rodent.

My Contribution

  • Trained and diagnosed reinforcement-learning policies across 237 experimental runs
  • Systematically varied 17 gap widths from 12 to 28 cm to characterize the task boundary
  • Designed a correct-rejection reward and dense-to-sparse reward schedules for uncrossable-gap trials
  • Added Gaussian noise to visual observations and varied starting positions; evaluations covered 6 noise levels, 3 visual conditions, and 3 starting distances
  • Used controlled evaluation changes to distinguish learning failures from evaluation artifacts

Research Context

Summer Undergraduate Research Fellow in the Talmo Lab and Salk Center for AI and Research Computing, June–August 2026. Selected as 1 of 10 Salk SURF fellows.

The project shifted the question from whether the virtual rat could cross a gap to what information and reward structure supported the behavior. Gaussian visual-noise tests, starting-position changes, and uncrossable-gap conditions were used to investigate the strategy behind successful performance.

Presentation

Presented “Eliciting Deliberation Before a Gap Jump in a Virtual Rat” at the Salk SURF Capstone and Talmo Lab research meeting in August 2026.

Neural Oscillation and Social Interaction

Question: How does ethanol alter VTA-PFC neural coordination during social interaction?

Approach: Analyzed approximately 8 hours of VTA-PFC LFP recordings from 24 mice using coherence, PAC, Granger causality, and mixed-effects models.

Result: Ethanol reduced theta-band VTA→PFC Granger causality while PFC→VTA connectivity remained comparatively stable.

Different questions.

Conceptual reading guide, not a data visualization. A and B are generic signals; arrows do not depict this study’s findings.

My Contribution

  • Analyzed VTA-PFC local field potential recordings from 24 mice using MATLAB and R
  • Integrated coherence, phase-amplitude coupling, and Granger causality with mixed-effects modeling
  • Quantified frequency-specific coupling and directional interactions between VTA and PFC
  • Compared ethanol and baseline conditions in the theta band (4–12 Hz)

Methods & Tools

  • Signal processing: Bandpass filtering, Hilbert transform, time-frequency decomposition
  • Metrics: Power spectral density (PSD), coherence, phase-amplitude coupling (PAC), Granger causality
  • Tools: MATLAB, Brainstorm, R, data visualization
  • Workflow: Event-aligned epoching, automated batch processing, reproducible pipelines

Key Findings

Identified reduced theta-band VTA→PFC Granger causality under ethanol exposure while PFC→VTA connectivity remained comparatively stable. Presented at the Wesleyan Summer Experience Presentation in July 2025 and Research Pathways Showcase in January 2026.

What made this work especially interesting was that integrating behavior with neural analyses required more than stacking methods together; I had to keep revising the pipeline so the metrics remained interpretable in the context of the experiment rather than drifting into abstraction.

Reproducibility

  • Data: Internal lab dataset (Melón Lab, Wesleyan University)
  • Code: Available upon request / private GitHub
  • Environment: MATLAB, Brainstorm, and R

Next Steps & Reflection

This project made directional communication between brain regions feel more informative than treating either region in isolation. It also showed me how strongly an interpretation depends on keeping signal processing tied to the behavioral and experimental context.

COVID-19 Cough Classification

Question: How far can cough-audio features support exploratory classification of COVID-19 status in a research setting?

Approach: A 430K-parameter CNN-BiGRU classifier with audio-quality filtering, augmentation, and spectral feature extraction across three public datasets.

Result: Across 5 folds and 6,624 samples, the model achieved 0.926 PR-AUC, 90.6% COVID-positive recall, and 0.892 F1.

My Contribution

  • Integrated 3 public respiratory-audio datasets
  • Built a Python pipeline for audio-quality filtering, augmentation, and spectral feature extraction
  • Developed a 430K-parameter CNN-BiGRU classifier
  • Evaluated the model across 5 folds and 6,624 samples under substantial class imbalance

Methods & Tools

  • Preprocessing: Audio-quality filtering, augmentation, and spectral feature extraction
  • Model: CNN-BiGRU with approximately 430K parameters
  • Validation: Five-fold evaluation using PR-AUC, recall, and F1
  • Tools: Python and TensorFlow
  • Focus area: Classification under substantial class imbalance

Achieved 0.926 PR-AUC, 90.6% COVID-positive recall, and 0.892 F1 across five folds and 6,624 samples.

One challenge here was learning not to trust aggregate performance at face value when the underlying data were substantially imbalanced.

Reproducibility

  • Training: AI4ALL Ignite AI Accelerator (Sep 2025 to Mar 2026)
  • Code: GitHub repository with version control

Boolean Genetic Networks

Question: How sensitive are genetic regulatory networks to mutations across different attractor states?

Approach: Simulated Kauffman Boolean networks with mutation sensitivity analysis on local machines.

Result: Explored attractor-dependent vulnerability patterns and the computational trade-offs involved in scaling the simulations.

My Contribution

  • Designed and implemented Boolean network simulations (Kauffman nets)
  • Developed mutation sensitivity analysis across different attractor states
  • Optimized memory usage and computational performance for larger local runs
  • Experimented with basic parallelization strategies on local hardware
Interactive / A minimal state cycle

Three nodes.
Follow one change.

Toy copy-rule network · not the study’s network or results

At each step, all nodes update together: A copies C, B copies A, and C copies B. Toggle a starting state, then follow it through time.

This changes an initial state, not a gene or update rule. It illustrates an attractor cycle—not biological propagation.

What I found compelling here was how quickly a simple model can become difficult to reason about at scale, which made computational efficiency and conceptual clarity feel like part of the same problem.

Methods & Tools

  • Model: Boolean genetic regulatory networks (Kauffman networks)
  • Analysis: Attractor identification, mutation sensitivity, robustness measures
  • Optimization: Memory-efficient data structures and exploratory parallel processing on local machines
  • Tools: Python, NumPy, computational biology modeling

Key Findings

Different attractor states showed varying degrees of sensitivity to mutations, suggesting that network topology and current state jointly determine robustness. Some attractors were remarkably stable, while others were highly vulnerable to single-node perturbations. This has implications for understanding biological robustness and evolvability.

Reproducibility

  • Code: Python simulation framework with configurable network parameters
  • Environment: Python 3.10, NumPy, local development environment

Next Steps & Reflection

This project taught me the importance of computational efficiency in large-scale simulations, even when running on local machines. What started as a straightforward modeling question became an exercise in thinking about optimization and potential future scaling. I'm interested in extending this approach to more realistic network topologies informed by empirical gene regulatory data, and exploring how network structure shapes evolutionary dynamics.
